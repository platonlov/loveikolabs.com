The fundamental difference: TrustScores vs dollars

This is the one comparison that matters most.

SuppCo uses proprietary "TrustScores" — composite ratings that combine several signals into a single number. The scoring formula is opaque; users have no way to verify why one supplement got a 7.2 and another got a 6.4. When SuppCo updates the formula, scores change without explanation.

StackSnap shows you actual dollars instead of scores. Every duplicate ingredient flagged, every potential overdose, every cheaper alternative discovered — quantified as money potentially saved. The underlying ingredient data sources directly to NIH DSLD entries (you can verify any data point against the public NIH database). No black-box ratings.

Which is better depends on what you want:

  • If you want a quick "is this supplement good?" score, TrustScores are simpler to read.
  • If you want to know why a supplement is or isn't worth your money — and how much you're wasting — the dollar approach is more actionable.

Where SuppCo is stronger

Massive review base and proven track record

18,780 ratings vs our 9. SuppCo has been tested across years and hundreds of thousands of users. If you weigh "battle-tested by many users" heavily, this is a real point in their favor. We're newer — there's less data on how StackSnap handles edge cases at scale.

Lower iOS requirement

iOS 15+ vs our 17+. If you have an iPhone 7 or 8 that can't update past iOS 15, SuppCo will install. StackSnap won't.

Brand recognition in the supplement-tracking community

If you've researched supplement apps in the last two years, you've probably heard of SuppCo. StackSnap is new — currently found mostly through recommendations and direct search rather than established word-of-mouth.

Polish from years of iteration

SuppCo has had two years to refine their UX. Some interface flows are more polished as a result. StackSnap is iterating fast, but at 9 reviews vs 18,780, the maturity gap is real.

Where StackSnap is stronger

Dollar savings counter (the main reason)

Every other supplement tracker shows scores or ratings. StackSnap quantifies your savings in actual money — duplicates avoided, overdoses caught, cheaper alternatives identified, low-evidence supplements flagged. The savings counter on the home screen is the single feature that justifies switching for most users. Average first-month savings: $50-$200.

RxNav-powered drug interaction checks

SuppCo doesn't check interactions with prescribed medications. StackSnap uses RxNav — the National Library of Medicine's drug interaction reference — to flag supplement-medication interactions automatically. For anyone taking any prescription alongside their supplements (which is most adults over 40), this is a genuinely important safety feature SuppCo lacks.

Source-verifiable ingredient data

StackSnap pulls every ingredient breakdown from the public NIH DSLD database. You can verify any data point against the public source directly. SuppCo's TrustScores are proprietary — you can't see how they're calculated.

Doctor PDF export

One-page summary of your stack with prescriptions, interactions, and timing — ready for any healthcare provider visit. SuppCo doesn't have this.

Honest 7-day free trial

Full Pro access for 7 days. No 20-question quiz before you see the app. No email signup gate. No surprise charges. The supplement-tracker category is notorious for dark-pattern paywalls — StackSnap is built deliberately as the antidote.

Lower annual price

$39.99 vs SuppCo's ~$69-79. Annual savings of $30-40 on top of the supplement savings the audit identifies.

Local-first storage (no data loss)

StackSnap uses SwiftData — your stack data lives on your iPhone first. SuppCo is cloud-only, which means logout or account problems can wipe access. (This is the original Care/of trauma that pushed many users to look for alternatives.)

Multi-marketplace pricing

StackSnap shows iHerb, Amazon Health, and Vitacost prices side-by-side for the same supplement. SuppCo doesn't surface where else you could buy what you're taking.

If you're currently using Prove It and looking for an honest alternative, StackSnap was built with that audience specifically in mind: real 7-day free trial, no quiz, transparent pricing, no email gate before you can use the app. The "honest supplement tracker" positioning is a direct response to the dark patterns that killed Prove It's recent reviews.
