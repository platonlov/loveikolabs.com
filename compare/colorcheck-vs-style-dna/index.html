ColorCheck vs Style DNA: an honest comparison.

Both are iOS AI color analysis apps. Style DNA launched in 2018 and is the category leader by review count (7,099 ratings). ColorCheck launched in April 2026 with a different focus — the BUY/SKIP scanner for individual items, multi-photo averaging for stable seasons, and a savings counter that quantifies money saved. Here's the honest comparison.

The fundamental difference: palette vs verdict

This is the comparison that matters most.

Style DNA determines your color season from a selfie and returns a color palette plus styling content. The output is the palette — abstract colors arranged in a grid. Implementation includes browsing-style features (outfit recommendations, virtual styling, lookbooks).

ColorCheck uses the same color season determination as the entry point but is built around a different core feature: the in-store BUY/SKIP scanner for individual clothing items. You point your camera at a garment in Zara, get a 3-second verdict ("Olive Green, 87% match, BUY"), and decide. The palette is necessary but not sufficient — the scanner is the actual product.

The categorical question this raises: do you want a color analysis app that gives you a palette and styling content (Style DNA), or one that gives you decision support on individual items in stores (ColorCheck)? Both are legitimate. They solve different problems.

Where Style DNA is stronger

Massive review base and proven track record

7,099 ratings vs our 11. Style DNA has been refined across 8 years and hundreds of thousands of users. If you weigh "battle-tested by many users" heavily, this is a meaningful advantage. We're new — there's less data on edge cases.

Styling content and outfit recommendations

Style DNA invests heavily in the "what to wear" content layer — outfit recommendations based on your palette, lookbooks for various occasions, virtual styling features. ColorCheck is deliberately narrower — we solve the BUY/SKIP decision and provide the palette, but we don't generate outfits or lookbooks. For users who want a more comprehensive "fashion assistant" experience, Style DNA is more complete.

Lower iOS requirement

iOS 15+ vs our 17+. If you have an iPhone 7 or 8 that can't update to iOS 17, Style DNA will install. ColorCheck won't.

Brand recognition

If you've researched color analysis apps before, you've probably encountered Style DNA. They're the established brand in the category. ColorCheck is new — currently found mostly through recommendations and direct search.

Polish from years of iteration

Eight years of UX refinement shows. Some Style DNA flows are smoother as a result of accumulated user feedback. ColorCheck is iterating fast, but the maturity gap is real.

Where ColorCheck is stronger

The in-store BUY/SKIP scanner (the main reason)

The core thing ColorCheck does that Style DNA doesn't: you point your camera at the specific olive blouse you're holding in Zara, and get a 3-second verdict on that specific garment. Style DNA tells you "your palette includes warm greens" — true, but doesn't help you decide whether this particular olive is warm enough. ColorCheck does.

Multi-photo averaging for stable seasons

Style DNA uses a single selfie. Lighting variation in a single selfie is the main reason users get different season results from different apps. ColorCheck averages across 3 photos (selfie + eyes + wrist), producing more stable, repeatable results. A 23-person study found single-photo binary AI color apps return correct results only 26% of the time. The multi-photo approach addresses both failure modes.

Confidence score (not binary)

Style DNA gives you a season. ColorCheck gives you a season plus a confidence score — and if confidence is below 70%, explicitly recommends re-analysis with better lighting. Transparency about uncertainty is the right scientific approach to a probabilistic question.

Dollar savings counter

Every SKIP scan adds to a visible monthly counter — "Saved $341 this month." The financial ROI is visible on the home screen. Style DNA doesn't quantify savings.

Closet audit with positive framing

Scan your existing closet for KEEP/DONATE verdicts with a final report. Style DNA has limited closet features. ColorCheck's audit is faster and uses positive framing ("These 12 items are perfect for you") rather than negative ("Donate these 8").

Honest 7-day free trial

Full Pro access. No 20-question quiz before you see the app. No email signup gate. Clear close button on the subscription screen. Style DNA uses a quiz-then-paywall flow which is more friction.

Lower annual price

$39.99 vs Style DNA's ~$69-99. The price difference compounds over years.

Higher current rating

4.91★ vs ~4.5★. Caveat: ours is from 11 reviews vs their 7,099 — small samples have higher variance. The gap may narrow as ColorCheck's review count grows.

Which one to choose

Choose ColorCheck if you:

  • Want decision support on specific clothing items, not just an abstract palette
  • Shop in physical stores (Zara, H&M, Anthropologie, thrift) and want in-store verdicts
  • Have been frustrated by different apps giving you different color seasons
  • Want to see your savings quantified in dollars
  • Are tired of quiz-before-paywall apps and want a clean 7-day trial
  • Have iPhone 11 or later running iOS 17+
  • Want the lowest annual subscription cost

Choose Style DNA if you:

  • Use an older iPhone that can't run iOS 17
  • Want extensive outfit recommendations and styling content (not just decision support)
  • Strongly value the largest review base in the category
  • Don't mind paying $30+/year more for established polish
  • Prefer browsing lookbooks and outfit ideas over scanning items in stores

Try them both

Both apps offer free access (ColorCheck 7-day full-Pro trial, Style DNA quiz + limited trial). Download both. Take the color analysis with each. If you get the same season from both apps with high confidence, the BUY/SKIP scanner is the actual deciding feature — and ColorCheck wins that comparison cleanly.
