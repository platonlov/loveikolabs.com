PestSnap vs Picture Insect — A Fair Comparison

Picture Insect, made by Glority, is the most-installed bug identifier on the App Store. PestSnap, made by us, is a newer entrant built around four scan modes and a homeowner verdict instead of a naturalist's encyclopedia entry. Same camera, different job. Here is how to choose.

Disclosure: we build PestSnap. We tried to write this comparison the way we would want a competitor to write one about us — with Picture Insect's strengths up front, not buried.

The 60-second verdict

Pick Picture Insect if you are a gardener, hiker, photographer, parent of a curious 8-year-old, or anyone who picks up insects out of interest and wants to know the species — common name, scientific name, life cycle, habitat, fascinating fact. Picture Insect's database is enormous (10,000+ species globally), its naturalist content is rich, and Glority has been refining it since 2019.

Pick PestSnap if you are a homeowner, renter, parent, or pet owner who just saw something move in your house and need to know — in plain English — whether to ignore it, vacuum it up, or call an exterminator. PestSnap covers a tighter list (25+ US home pests) but returns a verdict (harmless / nuisance / infestation risk) and the next step. It also has three modes Picture Insect does not have: Scan the Bite, Scan the Evidence, and Scan on Pet.

Pick both if you want both a learning tool and a practical home tool. The two apps barely overlap in actual job-to-be-done.

Where Picture Insect clearly wins

Picture Insect has had years to build something PestSnap does not have and is not trying to have: a global insect encyclopedia. If you are walking in the woods, take a photo of a beautiful longhorn beetle, and want to know what it is, when it is active, what plants it depends on, and whether it is native or invasive — Picture Insect is built for that. Its species coverage is roughly 400 times broader than ours.

Picture Insect also has a more developed gallery and sharing experience, an established community, and a free tier with limited daily identifications — useful if you only want to ID one bug a week and do not want to commit to a subscription.

For a naturalist or a curious kid, Picture Insect is the better app, and PestSnap is not trying to displace it.

Where PestSnap wins

PestSnap was designed for four moments Picture Insect does not address.

  1. You see a bug in your house and want a verdict, not a species name. When you find something on the kitchen counter at 11 p.m., "Periplaneta americana" is not what you need. "American cockroach — moderate infestation risk, here are three traps that work, here is how to find an exterminator" is what you need. PestSnap returns the second; Picture Insect returns the first.
  2. The bug is gone but the bites are on your skin. Scan the Bite uses the pattern, distribution, and welt characteristics on skin to estimate what bit you — bed bug, mosquito, flea, tick, or spider. This is the single highest-anxiety moment for renters and parents, and Picture Insect does not have this mode.
  3. You never saw the bug — only what it left behind. Scan the Evidence identifies droppings, mud tubes, frass, and other physical traces. This is often more diagnostic than seeing a single live bug, because the evidence tells you whether there is a population.
  4. The parasite is on your dog or cat. Scan on Pet is calibrated for ticks, fleas, lice, and mites on fur. Picture Insect can identify a tick if you pull it off and photograph it on white paper, but it does not understand pet context — and does not link to Pet Poison Helpline.

If price is the deciding factor and you are choosing on annual commitment, Picture Insect wins by about $10/year. If you can only commit weekly, PestSnap wins by $5/week. Most buyers do not pick on a $10 annual difference.

When neither app is enough

Both apps are AI-vision tools — neither is a medical or professional pest control service. Both will be wrong sometimes. The situations where you should put the app down:

  • Severe bite reaction. Difficulty breathing, throat swelling, widespread hives, fever, bullseye rash, fluid-filled blister, or any rapid spread. Call 911 or Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) immediately. Do not stop to take a photo.
  • Confirmed termite damage. If you see mud tubes, hollow-sounding wood, or swarmers in spring — call a licensed termite specialist for an inspection. Both apps will tell you "termite — likely subterranean", but neither can scope the damage or quote treatment.
  • Bed bug infestation. A single bed bug is identifiable. An actual infestation needs heat treatment or chemical treatment by a licensed pro. DIY does not work reliably for bed bugs.
  • Suspected brown recluse or black widow exposure with bite symptoms. Call Poison Control regardless of what either app says.

Both PestSnap and Picture Insect handle this honestly — neither claims to replace a professional. PestSnap surfaces Poison Control and the exterminator finder more prominently, because it is built for the worried homeowner moment rather than the curious naturalist moment.

The decision in one sentence

If you want to learn about a bug, get Picture Insect. If you want to deal with a bug, get PestSnap.
