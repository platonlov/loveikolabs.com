BumpCheck vs Little Bean — A Fair Comparison

Little Bean is the most-installed pregnancy ingredient checker on the US App Store with around 123,000 users, and a mature, well-reviewed cosmetic-checker experience. BumpCheck is a newer entrant covering cosmetics plus medications plus food with four input modes and a trimester-aware system. Same audience, broader scope. Here is how to choose.

Disclosure: we build BumpCheck. We tried to write this comparison the way we would want a competitor to write one about us — Little Bean's strengths up front, not buried at the bottom.

The 60-second verdict

Pick Little Bean if your job is cosmetic and skincare ingredients, you want the most mature and most-reviewed pregnancy app in the App Store, and you do not need medication or food guidance in the same place. Little Bean has been in active development since 2021, has thousands of positive reviews from real pregnant users, and is the safest "default" choice in this category. Camera-based label scanning is excellent.

Pick BumpCheck if you want one app that covers cosmetics plus over-the-counter medications (Tylenol, Benadryl, Claritin) plus food (mercury in fish, listeria categories) — all with the same trimester-aware reference system. The fastest paths in BumpCheck are barcode scanning for in-store decisions and the medication search via NIH RxNav for the 2 a.m. cold-and-flu moment.

Pick both if you can. The two apps cover meaningfully different ground. The honest truth is that many users keep Little Bean for its cosmetic ingredient library and add BumpCheck for the medication and food coverage Little Bean does not have.

Where Little Bean clearly wins

Little Bean has had years to refine something BumpCheck does not have yet: a mature, well-reviewed, install-base-tested experience focused exclusively on cosmetic ingredients. Their database of around 90 curated ingredient categories is reviewed by an in-house toxicologist, their alternative-product recommendations are well-developed, and their camera-based label scanning is excellent. With approximately 123,000 users and thousands of positive App Store reviews from real pregnant women, Little Bean is the safest "default" choice if you only need cosmetic ingredient checking and want a tool that has been around long enough to have ironed out the edge cases.

If your job is checking your Sephora skincare routine and you do not need medication or food coverage, Little Bean is the better-established app and BumpCheck is not trying to displace it.

Where BumpCheck wins

BumpCheck was designed for four moments Little Bean does not address.

  1. You are sick in the first trimester and need to know if Tylenol is okay. Little Bean does not cover medications. BumpCheck searches NIH RxNav and LactMed — the same databases US pharmacies and hospitals reference — and returns educational context on acetaminophen, ibuprofen, antihistamines, antacids, and most common OTC drugs. This is the highest-pain moment of pregnancy for most users, and it is the one Little Bean does not solve.
  2. You are at the fish counter and want to know if albacore tuna is the right pick. Little Bean does not cover food. BumpCheck references ACOG mercury content categories for fish, CDC listeria risk categories for soft cheeses and deli meats, and general dietary guidance for caffeine, herbal teas, and raw foods.
  3. You are at CVS and need a 5-second answer on a product with a barcode. Little Bean intentionally does not have a barcode scanner — they argue that the limited size of any barcode catalog produces false negatives. BumpCheck has the barcode scanner anyway, because for the 30-50% of common drugstore products that are in standard product databases, barcode scanning is dramatically faster than photographing a curved tube of cream.
  4. You want a single source of truth for your whole pregnancy. Three apps (Little Bean for cosmetics, MommyMeds for meds, some other reference for food) creates confusion. BumpCheck consolidates all three into one app with one trimester setting that adjusts the entire reference set.

Where Little Bean and BumpCheck disagree on philosophy

Two thoughtful disagreements between the apps that you should be aware of:

Barcode scanning. Little Bean has publicly argued against barcode scanners on the grounds that the underlying product catalogs (typically Open Food Facts and similar) cover only a fraction of products on shelves — so a "no concerning ingredients" verdict from a barcode lookup can hide ingredients the system did not see. BumpCheck's position is that barcode scanning is the fastest possible workflow when a product is in the database, and the right way to handle the gap is to always make label-photo scanning the second option with one tap. Both perspectives have merit.

Curated database vs aggregated public sources. Little Bean uses a curated database of about 90 ingredient categories reviewed by their in-house toxicologist. BumpCheck aggregates and surfaces the publicly-available sources directly (EWG, CIR, NIH, ACOG, CDC, FDA) with citations. The Little Bean approach produces tighter, more opinionated assessments; the BumpCheck approach produces more sources you can verify yourself. Pregnant users on Reddit consistently split on which they prefer.

When neither app is enough

Both apps are educational reference tools — neither is a medical service, neither makes medical recommendations, neither replaces your OB-GYN. The situations where the right answer is always to call your OB-GYN regardless of what either app says:

  • Any medication question that is not about a very routine OTC product. Even with acetaminophen (the most-researched pregnancy medication), dosing, frequency, and your specific medical context matter more than any app's general reference.
  • Any concerning symptom — bleeding, severe abdominal pain, fever above 100.4°F, severe nausea preventing fluids, decreased fetal movement after week 20.
  • Any prescription medication change — never adjust prescription medications during pregnancy based on app information alone.
  • Any pre-existing condition affecting your pregnancy — thyroid issues, diabetes, autoimmune conditions, mental health medications — all need OB-GYN guidance regardless of any app's verdict.
  • Anything that genuinely feels alarming — your OB-GYN's nurse line is the right next step, not Google and not an app.

For medical emergencies during pregnancy, call your OB-GYN, 911, or Poison Control (1-800-222-1222). Neither BumpCheck nor Little Bean is a substitute for emergency care.

The decision in one sentence

If you only need cosmetic ingredients with the most mature consumer app in this category, pick Little Bean. If you want cosmetics plus medications plus food in one trimester-aware app, pick BumpCheck. If you can afford both, both are useful for different jobs.
