💊 Use case · For wellness enthusiasts and complex stackers

How to audit your supplement stack and save $50-$200/month.

Seventy-seven percent of American adults take supplements. The average household spends $80-$200 per month, and most of those households have at least one duplicate, one overdose, and one supplement that doesn't do what the label implies. A 20-minute audit of your shelf usually finds $50-$200 in monthly savings. Here's the workflow.

What an "audit" actually means here

This isn't about telling you which supplements to take — that's a question for your doctor or registered dietitian based on your specific health needs. The audit is purely about finding inefficiencies in what you're already taking:

  • Duplicates — two products containing the same active ingredient (often unintentionally)
  • Overdoses — total daily intake above tolerable upper limits, where more isn't better and may cause harm
  • Drug interactions — supplements that interact with your prescriptions in ways your prescriber may not have flagged
  • Brand premium — paying significantly above market median for the same ingredient available from less-marketed brands
  • Low-evidence supplements — products with no published clinical evidence for the benefit claimed on the label

Acting on any of these is your decision. The audit just makes the information visible.

The 20-minute audit workflow

  1. Pull every supplement bottle off your shelf — every single one

    Don't skip ones you take occasionally. Don't skip the one you bought six months ago and forgot about. Lay them all out on a flat surface. Count them. Many people are surprised — "I thought I took maybe six, it's actually eleven."

  2. Open StackSnap and scan each bottle one at a time

    Front label and supplement facts panel both visible. Barcode scanning works for most US-marketed products in the NIH DSLD database. For uncommon or imported supplements, manual ingredient entry works too. Average scan time: under 10 seconds per bottle.

  3. Watch the savings counter populate as duplicates are flagged

    The home screen tallies in real time as you scan. Common findings on the first pass: your multivitamin's magnesium overlaps with your separate magnesium glycinate, your B-complex contains 100% RDA of B12 while your sublingual contains 16,000% RDA, your "joint blend" duplicates the glucosamine you take separately.

  4. Add your prescriptions for the interaction check

    If you take any prescription medication, add it now (drug name search or scan the prescription label). The RxNav-powered checker flags any interactions with what you've scanned — based on the same database your pharmacist uses. Common flags: fish oil + warfarin, St. John's Wort + SSRIs, turmeric + blood thinners, magnesium + certain antibiotics, vitamin K + warfarin.

  5. Review the benchmark pricing for top ingredients

    For 25 popular supplements, StackSnap shows whether your brand is above, at, or below the U.S. median price per serving. The brand premium is sometimes $10-30/month on a single supplement — often justified for specific quality reasons, sometimes not.

  6. Export the doctor PDF for your next healthcare visit

    One-page summary of every supplement, your prescriptions, any flagged interactions, and your daily schedule. Bring it to your next physical, pharmacy consultation, or specialist visit. Saves 5-10 minutes of fumbling, lets your provider actually engage with the information rather than scrambling to write it down.

What this guide is and isn't: this is a method for finding inefficiencies and potential safety issues in a supplement stack. It is not medical advice. Common audit findings, by stack type

The "wellness influencer" stack

Typical profile: 8-12 supplements influenced by Andrew Huberman, Peter Attia, Rhonda Patrick, and Tim Ferriss podcasts. Most common duplicates: omega-3 in fish oil + algae oil + grass-fed beef tallow blend. Most common overdose: B-vitamin complex + B12 sublingual + greens powder with B-vitamins. Most common low-evidence purchase: "longevity blend" containing 12 substances with no peer-reviewed studies for the combination. Average monthly savings: $80-150.

The "concerned about getting older" stack

Typical profile: 6-10 supplements for general healthy aging. Most common duplicates: multivitamin + bone health (both with vitamin D and K), heart health blend + fish oil (both with omega-3). Most common low-evidence purchase: collagen supplements (debated efficacy beyond placebo for most claims). Average monthly savings: $40-90.

The "newly diagnosed" stack

Typical profile: 5-8 supplements added after a new diagnosis (T2D, Hashimoto's, MTHFR variant, hypertension). Most common issue: not duplicates (the stack is intentional), but interaction risks with prescribed medications. Highest-value use of the audit: the doctor PDF for the next specialist visit. Savings less critical than interaction safety.

The "spreadsheet user" stack

Typical profile: 10-20 supplements, methodically tracked in Notion or Google Sheets, often built from biohacker protocols. Most common finding: tracking is good, but the user is missing the interaction layer (RxNav lookups across 20 supplements are impractical manually). The savings counter often surprises them — even meticulous spreadsheet users miss the same duplicates everyone else does.

What to actually do with the findings

Suggested order of action after the audit:

  1. Address any flagged interactions first. If StackSnap flags a supplement-medication interaction, schedule a pharmacy consultation (most chain pharmacies offer free 10-minute reviews) or message your doctor. Don't make changes alone.
  2. Drop confirmed duplicates that are causing overdoses — especially fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) where chronic overdose has cumulative effects.
  3. Discuss low-evidence supplements with your provider if they're expensive. "Memory blend" nootropics with seven ingredients are the most common find here.
  4. Switch brand premium where benchmarks are clearly above median and the ingredient quality isn't differentiated — these are pure cost reductions with no health impact.
  5. Set up smart reminders for what you decide to keep, so the timing aligns with absorption profiles (fat-soluble with meals, B-vitamins in the morning, magnesium at night).

The takeaway

Most people on supplements are spending more than they realize on things doing less than they think. The audit isn't about deprivation — it's about clarity. After 20 minutes, you know exactly what's in your stack, what overlaps, what costs more than it should, and what interacts with your prescriptions. Whether you change anything is your decision. But you can't change what you can't see.
