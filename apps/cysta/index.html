Overview

What is Cysta AI?

Cysta AI is a PCOS tracking app for people with polycystic ovary syndrome who want symptoms, cycles, food, weight, fertility context and bloodwork in one place — and a clearer summary to bring to their doctor.

PCOS is not one symptom. It is irregular or absent periods, acne, unwanted hair growth or hair thinning, weight changes, insulin resistance, and — for many people — questions about fertility. Generic period trackers are built around a predictable 28-day cycle; PCOS cycles often are not predictable. Cysta AI is built around PCOS specifically, so irregular cycles are the expected case, not an error.

It is organizational, not clinical. Cysta AI does not diagnose PCOS, does not prescribe metformin, inositol, spironolactone or any treatment, and does not replace a gynecologist or endocrinologist. It helps you keep an honest record and prepare better questions — the decisions stay with you and your clinician.

What makes it different: most PCOS apps are either a meal-plan program or a cycle predictor. Cysta AI is a tracker first — cycles, symptoms, meals, weight, supplements, a photo journal for visible changes, TTC signs and lab panels in one timeline — with an AI Coach that reads your recorded context and can show the sources behind its educational answers.

Workflow

How to track PCOS in a way your doctor can actually use — a 90-day framework

PCOS management is measured in months, not days. This is the workflow Cysta AI is built around.

  1. Day 1 — set your baseline.Log where you are today: last period date, current symptoms, weight, supplements and any medication your doctor has already prescribed. If you have recent bloodwork, scan it in. A baseline is what makes month three meaningful.
  2. Weeks 1–4 — log lightly, every day.A short daily check-in beats a detailed weekly one. Symptoms, meals, flow or spotting, and anything unusual — sleep, stress, a new supplement. Use reminders so the habit survives a busy week.
  3. Weeks 4–8 — start asking questions.Once there are a few weeks of data, ask the AI Coach about what you see: "Do my symptoms cluster around certain meals?" "What does research say about inositol and cycle regularity?" Treat answers as education and material for your next appointment.
  4. Month 2–3 — review trends, add photos and labs.Look at cycle history, symptoms, meals, weight and photo-journal entries together. If your clinician ordered follow-up labs, add them next to the first panel.
  5. Before the appointment — export the PDF.Pick the records that matter and export a doctor-ready summary. Bring your questions. A clinician can do far more with 90 days of organized records than with "my periods are kind of irregular".

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is Cysta AI?

Cysta AI is a PCOS tracking app for iPhone. It keeps PCOS symptoms, irregular cycles, meals, weight, supplements, a photo journal, TTC context and lab results in one place, adds an evidence-informed AI Coach that uses your recorded context, and exports a doctor-ready PDF. It is for informational, educational and organizational purposes only.

Can Cysta AI diagnose PCOS?

No. Cysta AI is not a medical device and does not diagnose or treat PCOS. PCOS is diagnosed by a clinician, typically using criteria that combine cycle history, signs or blood tests for elevated androgens, and ultrasound findings, after ruling out other causes. Cysta AI helps you organize the information that conversation needs.

Does it work if my periods are very irregular or absent?

Yes — that is the main reason it exists. Cysta AI records what actually happens (flow, spotting, gaps) rather than assuming a regular cycle, so long or absent cycles show up accurately in your history. Absent periods for three months or more are worth raising with a clinician.

What does the AI Coach do?

You can ask about PCOS symptoms, diet, insulin resistance, hormones, cycle changes, fertility and everyday routines. The Coach uses the context you recorded, organizes educational guidance and can show supporting sources. It is designed to help you understand patterns and prepare questions, not to make medical decisions for you.

Can I track lab results for PCOS?

Yes. Scan a bloodwork report or enter values manually, and Cysta AI keeps each panel organized next to your symptom and cycle history. Interpretation of lab values for your situation belongs to your clinician; the app keeps them in one place and helps you prepare questions.

Is Cysta AI useful when trying to conceive?

Yes. Track TTC context and ovulation signs alongside your cycle history and see an estimated fertile window. With irregular PCOS cycles, any fertile window estimate is less certain — it is a planning aid, not a guarantee and not a contraceptive method. A fertility specialist can advise on ovulation testing and treatment.

How is Cysta AI different from Flo or Clue?

Flo and Clue are excellent general cycle trackers with very large user bases. Cysta AI is narrower: it is built around PCOS, keeps lab results and a photo journal for visible changes in the same record, and includes an AI Coach that reads your own logs. Many people with PCOS use a general tracker for years before switching to something PCOS-specific.

How is Cysta AI different from PCOS Pal or Cysterhood?

PCOS Pal and Cysterhood are structured programs — lessons, meal plans, recipes, workouts and coaching. Cysta AI is a tracker and organizer: it does not give you a meal plan, but it records what you eat, how you feel, your cycles and your labs in one timeline for you and your clinician. Some people use a program and a tracker together.

What happens to my photos and lab images?

Cysta AI requests clear consent before AI features process Coach messages, photos or lab images. See the privacy policy linked in the App Store listing for full detail on storage and processing.

How much does Cysta AI cost?

In the US, Cysta AI Pro is $7.99 per week with a 7-day free trial for eligible new subscribers, or $39.99 per year (no trial). Prices vary by storefront and are shown before purchase. Subscriptions auto-renew unless cancelled at least 24 hours before the period ends.

When should I see a doctor instead of using an app?

See a clinician for a PCOS diagnosis or treatment decisions, periods absent for three months or more, very heavy bleeding, severe pelvic pain, signs of pregnancy complications, or rapid unexplained weight change. For severe pain, heavy bleeding with dizziness or fainting, call 911 or your local emergency number.
