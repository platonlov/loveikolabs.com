What is BumpCheck?

BumpCheck is an iPhone reference tool for pregnant and breastfeeding women. You scan a barcode, photograph an ingredient label, paste an ingredient list, or search a medication — and the app returns trimester-aware educational context drawn from publicly available public health research. It is an educational tool, not a medical service: it does not diagnose, prescribe, or recommend a course of treatment. Every result page in the app says so. The job BumpCheck is built for is the moment a pregnant woman is standing in Sephora at 11 p.m. with a retinol moisturizer in her hand and wants a 5-second answer instead of opening 4 browser tabs.

Four ways to check

The fastest way to lose a pregnant user is to make her type. BumpCheck has four input modes so you can use whichever is closest at hand.

  • Scan a barcode — point the camera at any product's UPC code and BumpCheck looks up the full ingredient list and runs each ingredient against the sources for your current pregnancy stage. This is the in-store mode — built for the moment you have 30 seconds before the staff asks if you need help.
  • Photograph an ingredient label — when there is no barcode, or the product is an indie brand not in any database. The camera reads the printed text directly using AI vision. Works on labels in any language with Latin characters.
  • Paste an ingredient list or product name — for online shopping. Copy the ingredients from a Sephora or Amazon listing and paste into BumpCheck.
  • Search medications by brand or generic name — Tylenol, Benadryl, Claritin, Tums, Zofran, or their generic equivalents. The app queries NIH RxNav and NIH LactMed — the same databases US healthcare professionals use.

Trimester-aware educational context

Research on many ingredients differs by pregnancy stage. The same retinoid that is documented as a concentrated research concern in T1 (during organogenesis) has a different context in breastfeeding. NSAIDs have specific T3 concerns documented by ACOG. BumpCheck adjusts the educational context to your current stage so you see what is relevant to where you are now.

Six stages are supported:

  • Trying to Conceive (TTC) — early-stage caution before confirmed pregnancy
  • First Trimester — weeks 1-13, organogenesis window, highest sensitivity for many ingredients
  • Second Trimester — weeks 14-27, generally lower-risk context for many topical products
  • Third Trimester — weeks 28-40, ACOG-documented restrictions on certain medication classes
  • Postpartum — recovery window, different considerations
  • Breastfeeding — NIH LactMed-based context, often different from pregnancy context

You can change your stage at any time in Settings, and previous scans update their context automatically.

Public sources we reference

BumpCheck does not generate medical opinions. It aggregates and presents publicly available research from authoritative sources:

  • CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention) — food safety guidance, vaccine guidance
  • ACOG (American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists) — pregnancy clinical guidelines
  • NIH RxNav — drug information database, the same one US pharmacies and hospitals use
  • NIH LactMed — breastfeeding compatibility database maintained by the National Library of Medicine
  • EWG Skin Deep — cosmetic ingredient assessments covering 70,000+ products
  • CIR (Cosmetic Ingredient Review) — independent industry-standard cosmetic safety assessments
  • FDA — drug information, food safety, supplement regulations

If a source disagrees with another, BumpCheck shows you both. The point of the app is not to give you a single answer — it is to give you the same information your OB-GYN would reference in 90% of cases, in 5 seconds instead of 30 minutes.

Who BumpCheck is built for

Four overlapping audiences keep us focused on what to build next.

  • First-time mothers in their twenties and thirties who want peace of mind without spending two hours per question on Google. The 2 a.m. retinol-or-acne-cream decision is the highest-leverage moment.
  • Skincare-routine builders who had a 10-step routine before pregnancy and now need to know which of their existing products to keep, which to swap, and what to buy as alternatives.
  • Sick-pregnant users in cold-and-flu season — first trimester plus a sinus infection plus questions about Tylenol, Benadryl, Tums, and Mucinex. This is the highest-pain audience and the highest-conversion segment.
  • Second-time moms and breastfeeding women who already know what they want — they buy the annual or bundle tier and use the app across multiple pregnancies and feeding cycles.

Frequently asked questions

Is BumpCheck medical advice?

No. BumpCheck provides educational information drawn from publicly available public health sources (CDC, ACOG, NIH, EWG, CIR, FDA) for informational purposes only. It is not a substitute for medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Every result page in the app says so, and the app always recommends consulting your OB-GYN or another qualified healthcare provider regarding any questions about pregnancy, breastfeeding, medications, cosmetics, or food. BumpCheck does not make medical recommendations.

Does BumpCheck tell me what is "safe" or "unsafe"?

It tells you the educational context published by authoritative sources. Where ACOG says NSAIDs are not recommended in T3, the app shows that with the citation. Where EWG rates an ingredient as low concern, the app shows that with the link to Skin Deep. We do not invent verdicts — we surface what the sources say. A small number of ingredients (retinoids in T1, isotretinoin, certain antiepileptics) have a strong research consensus to avoid; we present that consensus and the citations. For most ingredients the context is more nuanced, and we show both sides where they exist.

Can I trust the medication information?

The medication information comes from NIH RxNav and NIH LactMed — the same databases US pharmacies, hospitals, and many OB-GYNs reference. We do not generate medication guidance ourselves. That said, individual circumstances matter enormously in pregnancy medication decisions, and BumpCheck is not a substitute for your OB-GYN. Use it to know what your OB-GYN will likely say, then call them anyway for anything beyond very routine OTC questions.

How long does a scan take?

A barcode scan returns context in about 2-3 seconds. A label photo (AI vision text extraction) takes 5-8 seconds depending on image complexity. A paste or search is instant. The slowest path is roughly the time it takes you to put down your shopping bag.
