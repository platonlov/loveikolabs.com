Overview

What is Salvora?

Salvora is an AI eczema tracker for people living with atopic dermatitis, recurring flares, dry or itchy skin and changing care routines — and for parents organizing a child's eczema history.

Eczema is a pattern condition. The single most useful thing you can bring to a dermatologist is a history: when flares happened, how bad they were, what changed beforehand and what you used on your skin. Memory is terrible at this — especially at 2 a.m. with an itching child.

Salvora records that history in a few taps: flare dates; itch, rash, dryness and sleep loss; food, allergy, stress, weather, product and routine triggers; creams, moisturizers, topical steroids and baths. A POEM-style self-reported severity score turns "it's been bad lately" into a trend line. Photos build a skin timeline that stays on your phone.

AI context, not a diagnosis. Opt-in AI can summarize patterns across your logs and photos so they are easier to review. Salvora does not diagnose eczema, identify diseases, prescribe treatment or replace a clinician. It can show you the warning signs published by the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) and the National Eczema Association (NEA) and tell you when they warrant a call.

Capabilities

What Salvora tracks — and why each piece matters

  • Flares and flare dates. The backbone of any eczema history. Log when a flare starts and how it progresses.
  • Itch, rash, dry skin and sleep loss. The symptoms that shape daily life — including the nights lost to scratching.
  • Trigger log. Food, allergy, stress, weather, products and routines. Over time Salvora helps you compare your flare history with the context you recorded.
  • Care routine history. Creams, moisturizers, topical steroids, baths and other care steps — what you used, when, and how skin responded.
  • POEM-style severity trend. A self-reported severity score modeled on the style of POEM (Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure), tracked over weeks so change is visible.
  • Photo timeline on your iPhone. Dated photos and notes of your skin, stored on device. Slow change becomes visible; appointments become concrete.
  • Opt-in AI pattern summaries. When you choose, AI summarizes patterns across logs and photos so records are easier to review. Context, not diagnosis.
  • Doctor-ready summary + warning signs. Flare timeline, severity trend, symptom notes, trigger log, care history and photo timeline in one summary. AAD- and NEA-published warning signs show when to call a clinician.

Workflow

How to build an eczema history your dermatologist can use — a 6-week framework

Eczema changes over weeks. This is the rhythm Salvora is built around.

  1. Day 1 — baseline.Record today's skin: where it's affected, itch, sleep last night, and a POEM-style severity score. Take baseline photos of the affected areas. List your current care routine.
  2. Daily — a 30-second log.Itch, sleep, anything unusual (new product, stress, weather change, new food), and what you applied. Short and consistent beats detailed and occasional.
  3. When a flare starts — log it immediately.Mark the flare, add a photo, note what changed in the previous 48 hours. This is where trigger patterns come from.
  4. Weekly — severity score and review.Log a weekly POEM-style score and look at the trend. If AI summaries are on, review what patterns it highlights — as questions, not conclusions.
  5. Before the appointment — export.Export the doctor-ready summary. Bring questions about what the trend shows, which triggers seem to recur and how your routine could change.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is Salvora?

Salvora is an educational AI eczema tracker for iPhone. It records flares, itch, rash, sleep, foods, triggers and care routines, tracks a POEM-style severity trend, keeps a photo timeline on your iPhone and exports a doctor-ready summary.

Can Salvora diagnose eczema?

No. Salvora does not diagnose eczema, identify diseases, prescribe treatment or replace a clinician. Its optional AI summarizes patterns in your own logs and photos so they're easier to review.

What is POEM?

POEM (Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure) is a widely used questionnaire in which patients rate eczema symptoms such as itch, sleep disturbance, bleeding, weeping, cracking, flaking and dryness over the past week. Salvora uses a POEM-style self-reported severity score to show your trend over time; it is for personal tracking, not a clinical assessment.

Can Salvora find my eczema triggers?

It helps you compare your flare history with the context you recorded — foods, stress, weather, allergens, products and routines. Triggers become clearer over weeks of consistent logging. Suspected food allergies should be evaluated by an allergist rather than by elimination alone.

Can I use Salvora for my child?

Yes. Parents can organize a child's eczema history — flares, sleep disruption, foods, care routines and photos — and share the summary with a pediatrician, pediatric dermatologist or allergist.

Where are my photos stored?

Photos are stored on your iPhone. No account is required, AI analysis is opt-in, and there are no ads or third-party analytics.

EczemaWise is shutting down — can Salvora replace it?

EczemaWise has ended new account creation and existing users can continue only through December 18, 2026. Salvora covers the core jobs — flares, triggers, care routines, a severity trend and a doctor-ready summary — in a private, on-device record. Start with baseline photos and your current routine so your history continues.

How much does Salvora cost?

Salvora Pro is $39.99 per year or $7.99 per week in the US, each with a 7-day free trial for eligible Apple IDs. Both plans unlock the same features and auto-renew unless cancelled at least 24 hours before renewal.

When should I contact a clinician urgently?

Contact a clinician promptly for signs of skin infection — spreading redness, warmth, pus, yellow crusting, or painful clustered blisters (which can signal eczema herpeticum and needs same-day care) — or for fever, rapidly worsening skin, or a flare that doesn't respond to your usual plan. Call 911 or your local emergency number for allergic reactions with swelling of the face or throat or difficulty breathing.

Bring your dermatologist a history, not a guess.
