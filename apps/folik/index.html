What Folik Is

Folik is an educational companion app for adults experiencing hair thinning, hair loss, or pattern hair loss who want a tracking tool with cited sources instead of marketing language. It is not a medical device. It does not prescribe finasteride, dutasteride, minoxidil, or any medication. It does not diagnose alopecia areata, scarring alopecia, or any condition. It is a tracker plus a research-grounded reference layer that sits between dermatology visits, so the conversation with your dermatologist is more productive — not replaced.

What it is not. Folik is not a hair growth supplement promoter, a transplant clinic referral service, a telehealth pharmacy, or a snake-oil store. The AI Coach is grounded in AAD, ISHRS, FDA, and PubMed sources — not influencer recommendations or affiliate-loaded "Big 3" hot takes from Reddit threads. If you came looking for a magic shampoo recommendation, this is not the app.

Treatments Folik Tracks

Folik supports tracking for the treatments most commonly used in pattern hair loss management. We track them; we do not prescribe them.

  • Topical minoxidil 2% and 5% (Rogaine® and generics). OTC in the US. FDA-approved for androgenetic alopecia. Once or twice daily application typical.
  • Oral minoxidil. Prescription required. FDA-approved for hypertension, off-label for hair loss. Increasingly common in dermatology practice at low doses; consult prescriber.
  • Finasteride 1mg (Propecia® and generics). Prescription required. FDA-approved for male pattern hair loss. Not for women of childbearing potential (FDA Pregnancy Category X).
  • Finasteride 5mg (Proscar® generic). Prescription required. FDA-approved for BPH; sometimes used off-label at lower divided doses for hair loss.
  • Dutasteride (Avodart®). Prescription required. FDA-approved for BPH only; off-label for hair loss in the US. Approved for hair loss in some other countries.
  • Microneedling at home (dermaroller) or in-office. Generally safe with proper technique. Synergy with topical minoxidil documented in Dhurat et al. 2013.
  • Ketoconazole shampoo (Nizoral® and generics) — OTC at 1%, prescription at 2%.
  • Supplements — biotin, vitamin D, zinc, saw palmetto, iron (for women with low ferritin specifically). Track adherence; supplements are not substitutes for clinical treatments.
  • Low-level laser therapy (LLLT) — FDA-cleared devices for hair loss treatment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will Folik tell me if I should start finasteride?

No. The Coach surfaces what the AAD guidelines, FDA prescribing information, and peer-reviewed studies say about finasteride — including its mechanism (5α-reductase inhibition, DHT reduction), efficacy in published trials, the known side-effect profile, and the persistence concerns documented in post-marketing reports. Whether finasteride is right for you is a conversation between you and a dermatologist. We do not push you toward or away from any treatment.

Is the AI Coach a substitute for a dermatologist?

No. The Coach is an educational reference layer that cites publicly available sources — not a medical provider. For diagnosis (is this androgenetic alopecia, telogen effluvium, alopecia areata, or scarring alopecia?), treatment selection, prescription writing, and managing side effects, you need a dermatologist.

What about post-finasteride syndrome (PFS)?

The Coach surfaces the published evidence honestly. PFS is reported in the literature with persistence concerns that warrant attention. It is also a contested clinical area. The Coach does not minimize the reports nor dismiss them; it cites the sources and recommends that anyone considering finasteride discuss the documented post-marketing reports with their prescriber before starting, especially regarding baseline sexual function documentation and informed consent.

Can the AI Coach diagnose my hair loss type?

No. The Coach can surface what each pattern typically looks like (androgenetic alopecia, telogen effluvium, alopecia areata, scarring alopecia, traction alopecia, postpartum shedding) and what questions a dermatologist will likely ask during evaluation. Diagnosis requires clinical examination, often a pull test, sometimes a scalp biopsy or trichoscopy — none of which an app can replace.

I am a woman. Can I use Folik?

Yes. The staging calculator uses the Ludwig scale for women. The Coach surfaces female pattern hair loss research alongside male pattern hair loss research. Important: finasteride and dutasteride are FDA Pregnancy Category X and are not appropriate for women of childbearing potential — the Coach surfaces this explicitly. Female hair loss frequently involves contributors (postpartum telogen effluvium, PCOS, thyroid dysfunction, iron deficiency, restrictive diets) that benefit from medical evaluation rather than self-tracking alone.

Important Disclaimer

Folik is an educational companion app. It is not a medical device. It does not prescribe medication, recommend dose changes, diagnose conditions, or replace consultation with a dermatologist or other licensed healthcare provider. The AI Hair Loss Coach provides educational information based on publicly available sources (AAD guidelines, ISHRS resources, FDA prescribing information, peer-reviewed PubMed studies) — not personalized medical advice.

Always consult a dermatologist before starting or changing any hair loss treatment.

Seek medical attention for:

  • Sudden severe hair shedding (could indicate telogen effluvium triggered by an underlying medical issue — illness, surgery, severe stress, medication, thyroid dysfunction, iron deficiency, postpartum hormonal shift)
  • Patchy hair loss with smooth bald patches (possible alopecia areata, an autoimmune condition)
  • Hair loss with skin changes, scarring, itching, burning, or scaling (possible scarring alopecia)
  • Sexual side effects on finasteride or dutasteride — call your prescriber immediately
  • Severe scalp pain, signs of infection from microneedling — 911 or urgent care for severe symptoms
  • Severe allergic reaction to any treatment — call 911
  • Mental health crisis from body image concerns — call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline)

Finasteride and dutasteride are FDA Pregnancy Category X — pregnant women should not handle crushed or broken tablets. These medications can cause serious birth defects.
