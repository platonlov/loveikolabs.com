Overview

What is ColorCheck?

ColorCheck is an iPhone AI shopping guard that determines your precise 12-season color profile and then gives you a live BUY or SKIP verdict on any clothing item — in 3 seconds, in any store.

The problem it solves: clothes bought in the wrong color often go unworn. The dominant cause is not style — it is color. Wrong-undertone clothing makes your skin look sallow or ruddy. Wrong-depth clothing washes you out. Wrong-contrast clothing overwhelms your features. You typically cannot tell which is happening under store lighting (warm halogen, fluorescent, LED — all biased). ColorCheck is the 3-second diagnostic that closes that gap before you commit money.

The App Store has 18+ color analysis apps. Most give you a palette and stop there. ColorCheck is the only app that takes you from palette to per-garment verdict — the "I have a palette, now what?" problem that plagues the entire category.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How is ColorCheck different from other color analysis apps?

Every other color analysis app gives you an abstract palette and stops there. ColorCheck is the only app that gives you a BUY or SKIP verdict on the specific clothing item you're holding in the store. The palette is the entry point, not the product. After determining your color season from a 3-photo analysis, you point your camera at any garment and get a 3-second verdict — Olive Green 87% match BUY, or Mint Green 23% match SKIP with a suggested warmer alternative.

How accurate is the color season determination?

ColorCheck uses 3 photos in different lighting (selfie + close-up of eyes + wrist) and averages the results. Single-photo results are sensitive to lighting, which is why different apps often disagree. Multi-photo averaging plus confidence scoring addresses both failure modes. If your confidence score is below 70%, ColorCheck explicitly recommends a re-analysis with better lighting rather than committing to an uncertain season.

What's the 12-season color analysis system?

The 12-season system is the professional color analysis standard used by certified colorists worldwide. It extends the basic 4 seasons (Spring/Summer/Autumn/Winter) into 3 variations each:

  • Spring: Light Spring, True Spring, Bright Spring
  • Summer: Light Summer, True Summer, Soft Summer
  • Autumn: Soft Autumn, True Autumn, Deep Autumn
  • Winter: Cool Winter, True Winter, Deep Winter

It captures variation in undertone (warm/cool), contrast level (high/medium/low), and depth (light/medium/deep). Same framework professional stylists use in $200–800 consultations.

Why do I get different seasons from different apps?

Three reasons:

  • Single-photo apps are sensitive to lighting variation — a selfie in cool indoor light reads "cool" colors; the same person in warm window light reads "warm." Multi-photo averaging (ColorCheck's approach) fixes this.
  • Binary verdicts with no confidence score hide uncertainty. If you're truly on the boundary between Soft Autumn and Soft Summer, different apps will land on different sides. ColorCheck's confidence score makes this visible.
  • Training data differences — many apps trained primarily on light-skinned subjects perform poorly on darker skin tones. ColorCheck's training set covers all Fitzpatrick types.
What if my confidence score is low?

If confidence is below 70%, ColorCheck explicitly says so and recommends re-analysis. Common causes:

  • Photos taken in mixed lighting (window + indoor lamp)
  • Heavy makeup that masks your natural undertone
  • Hair color that doesn't match your natural color
  • Photos with strong filters or skin smoothing

Retake in natural daylight (window light, no direct sun), no makeup, hair tied back. The repeat analysis is free and unlimited during your Pro subscription.

How does the BUY or SKIP verdict work?

The scanner does three things:

  1. Identifies the specific shade of the garment (e.g. "Warm Olive Green")
  2. Compares it against your personal color palette to compute a match score (0–100%)
  3. Returns the verdict with a one-sentence explanation

80%+ match = BUY IT. 40–79% = neutral, your call (it won't hurt you but isn't optimal). Under 40% = SKIP IT with a suggested alternative in your palette.

Does ColorCheck work in any store lighting?

Yes, but accuracy improves in better lighting. Store lighting is typically biased — fitting rooms use warm halogen, fluorescent under-light, or fluorescent overhead. ColorCheck's scanner is calibrated to correct for common store lighting types. For best results in particularly bad lighting (very yellow incandescent, very blue LED), step closer to a window or store entrance.

Can ColorCheck scan online product photos?

Yes via screenshot. The screenshot scanner corrects for known store filter biases — Anthropologie photos are noticeably warm/sepia, ASOS leans neutral, Zara varies. The scanner adjusts for the known filter pattern of each major retailer. For unfamiliar retailers, the scanner uses general color correction but flags lower confidence on the verdict.

What about patterned or multi-color clothing?

For multi-color patterns, the scanner identifies the dominant color and gives a verdict on that. For complex patterns (florals, prints), it gives an "overall harmony score" — how well the combination of colors aligns with your palette. Generally: solid colors give the most reliable verdicts. Two-tone patterns work well. Complex prints are best evaluated by considering the dominant color individually.

How does the savings counter work?

Every time you scan an item in a store and get a SKIP verdict, the item's price tag value is added to your monthly savings counter. Home screen shows totals like "Saved $341 this month" based on actual purchases avoided. Most users find their savings exceed the annual subscription cost within the first 2–3 weeks. The counter creates visible ROI — wellness-tracking apps show streaks, ColorCheck shows dollars.

Will ColorCheck work for men, dark skin tones, or specific eye colors?

Yes. The 12-season color analysis system works across all skin tones, hair colors, and eye colors. Training data covers Fitzpatrick skin types I through VI. For darker skin tones specifically, the system relies more on undertone (warm/cool) and contrast level (high/medium/low) than on absolute skin color — the correct methodology used by professional colorists. The dominant user base is currently women aged 25–45, but the system is gender-neutral and works for all genders.

What if I don't like my color season?

This is more common than people expect — particularly with Soft Autumn (muted earthy palette) and Light Summer (very desaturated palette). Two things to remember:

  • Every season has rich and beautiful shades. Soft Autumn includes the deep rust of fall leaves, mossy greens, rich olives, warm taupes. The "muted" framing is misleading.
  • Match score is a spectrum, not binary. 60% match doesn't mean "you can't wear this" — it means "decent, not ideal."

The celebrity color twin feature helps here — when Jessica Alba is your example, "Warm Autumn" stops feeling drab and starts feeling like a real aesthetic.

ColorCheck's value is the in-store BUY/SKIP scanner for individual items, which no stylist can give you when you're standing in Zara at 7 PM. Many ColorCheck users already know their season; they use the app for the scanner only.
