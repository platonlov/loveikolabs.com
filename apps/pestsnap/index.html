What is PestSnap?

PestSnap is an iPhone bug identifier built for the moment you open a drawer and see something move. Point your camera at the bug, the bite on your arm, the droppings on the counter, or the tick on your dog — and within about three seconds you get a plain-English answer. Not a Latin name. Not a Wikipedia article. A clear verdict (harmless, nuisance, or infestation risk), three to five specific DIY methods you can do tonight, and a one-tap path to a licensed exterminator if you actually need one. PestSnap covers 25+ of the most common US household pests, runs entirely on your iPhone camera, and is not a medical or professional pest control service.

Four ways to scan

Most bug identifier apps are built for one thing: photographing a bug and naming it. PestSnap is built for four different situations a normal homeowner actually runs into.

  • Scan the Bug. Standard mode — point the camera at the bug itself. Roach on the counter, spider on the ceiling, ant trail by the dishwasher, wasp on the porch. Get an ID plus the threat level.
  • Scan the Bite. The bug is gone but the bites are not. Photograph the bite pattern on your skin and PestSnap matches it against documented bite patterns for bed bugs, mosquitoes, fleas, ticks, and spiders. This is the mode that wins parents and renters.
  • Scan the Evidence. You never saw the bug — only what it left behind. Roach droppings, mouse smear marks, mud tubes from termites, holes in the pantry. The app identifies what made them.
  • Scan on Pet. Photograph the parasite on your dog or cat — tick, flea, lice, or mite. Especially useful after a hike or a stay at a kennel.

Who PestSnap is built for

Four overlapping audiences keep us focused on what to ship next.

  • Homeowners. You bought the place and now you are responsible for what crawls inside it. PestSnap tells you whether the thing in the basement is a harmless cellar spider or a brown recluse — and whether the mud tubes near the porch are termite damage or just dirt.
  • Renters. Apartment pests are an emergency because you cannot just buy a $400 termite treatment and move on. You need to know in five minutes whether to email the landlord or just vacuum it up. Scan the Bug and Scan the Bite are made for the 2 a.m. moment when you think you might have bedbugs.

  • Pet owners. Anything attached to your dog after a hike is a question. Scan on Pet tells you whether to pull and dispose, or pull and call the vet.

Frequently asked questions

How does PestSnap actually identify a bug from a photo?

PestSnap sends your photo to a current-generation multimodal AI vision model fine-tuned for 25+ common US home pests. It compares visible features — body shape, segmentation, antenna pattern, leg count, wing structure, color and marking — against documented reference images. For bites it analyzes pattern, distribution, surrounding skin reaction. For evidence (droppings, mud tubes) it analyzes shape, size, and substrate. The result returns in about 3 seconds.

Will PestSnap work if I cannot get a clear photo?

It will try, and it will be honest about uncertainty. If the photo is blurry, partial, or shot from a bad angle, you will get a top three list with confidence levels rather than a single verdict. Practical tip: hold the camera 4-8 inches from the bug, tap the screen to lock focus on the body, and shoot in normal indoor light if possible. Avoid flash directly on shiny exoskeletons — it washes out the markings.

Does PestSnap diagnose medical conditions from bites?

No, and we are explicit about this. PestSnap identifies what likely bit you based on the pattern, but it is not a medical tool. It does not diagnose Lyme disease, allergic reactions, cellulitis, or anything else. If a bite has severe swelling, fever, a bullseye rash, spreading redness, or any allergic-reaction symptoms, call 911 or Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) and see a doctor. We surface those numbers directly in the app for exactly this reason.

What if PestSnap gets it wrong?

Even the best AI bug identifiers are not 100% accurate, and we do not claim to be. Misidentifications happen most often with edge species (rare regional pests, exotic species, larval stages where the adult looks completely different) and with poor photos. For an actual infestation — termites, bed bugs, anything where you are about to spend more than $200 on treatment — always confirm with a licensed exterminator's in-person inspection before paying.
