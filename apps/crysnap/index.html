What is CrySnap?

CrySnap is an iPhone app for new parents in the first six months — the peak crying phase, when sleep is broken and every cry feels like a puzzle. Tap record, the app captures 5-20 seconds of audio, and within about three seconds it returns the most likely meaning of the cry across nine documented categories: hungry, tired, discomfort, gas or burping, colic, pain, overstimulated, lonely, or undefined. Every result includes a confidence score. The cry classifier was trained on more than 10,000 infant cry recordings and reaches 89% test accuracy on the 9-class problem under research conditions. After the translation you can ask follow-up questions to one of three built-in AI assistants without leaving the app.

A team of three AI assistants — not just a cry translator

The core differentiator: every cry translation result has an "Ask more" option that hands you off to one of three specialists, each tuned for a different parenting question pattern.

  • The pediatric nurse. Ask anything about newborn care — diaper rashes, infant fever thresholds, normal vs concerning behavior, when to call your doctor. Trained on AAP (American Academy of Pediatrics) guidance. Every response includes "when to call your doctor" triggers when relevant.
  • The sleep coach. Wake windows by age, nap scheduling, the 4-month sleep regression, room conditions, swaddling, sleep training methods (Ferber, gentle, no-cry). Custom sleep plans based on your baby's patterns once you have logged a few days of data.
  • The lactation consultant. Breastfeeding latching issues, pumping schedules, cluster feeding, milk supply concerns, weaning. Non-judgmental about formula, combination feeding, or whatever works for your family.

Voice mode for the 3 a.m. moment

This is the feature parents underestimate until they use it once. At 3 a.m. when your baby is crying and you cannot open your eyes enough to read a screen, you do not want to type. Voice mode lets you tap one button on the lock screen and just talk — "she will not stop crying and she just ate forty minutes ago" — and the assistant responds out loud in seconds. Hands-free, eyes-closed, baby-in-arms. It is the same conversation you would have if you could reach a 24-hour pediatric nurse hotline, except the response is instant and you do not have to put on hold music.

Soothing sounds that work

The soothing-sounds library is not just labeled "white noise". Each sound is calibrated by frequency profile and has documented use cases:

  • White noise at multiple frequency bands — the higher-frequency mix mimics the in-utero blood-flow sound newborns recognize most.
  • Womb sounds for the first 6-8 weeks when fourth-trimester soothing is most effective.
  • Shushing scientifically calibrated to the volume level newborns actually need (which is louder than most parents intuit — closer to a vacuum cleaner than a whisper).
  • Lullabies in English, Spanish, French, and Mandarin.
  • Heartbeat rhythms at the BPM ranges that match a resting maternal heartbeat.

All sounds work offline and can be looped overnight without draining the battery.

Who CrySnap is built for

Four audience groups keep us focused.

  • New moms in the first six months. The peak crying phase, peak sleep deprivation, peak self-doubt. CrySnap turns "I have no idea what she wants" into a calm three-second verdict and a follow-up question you can ask the pediatric nurse.
  • Dads who want to help but feel lost. Especially in the first month. CrySnap evens the parenting floor — if you can record a cry and read the result, you can take a shift overnight.
  • Grandparents caring for grandchildren. Especially when guidelines have changed since they raised their own kids (back sleep, swaddle release at 8 weeks, peanut introduction at 4-6 months). The pediatric nurse is current on AAP guidance.
  • Anyone exhausted at 3 a.m. wondering "why is he still crying". This is the moment the whole app is built for.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the cry translation actually?

The underlying model reaches 89% test accuracy on the 9-class problem under research conditions — clean audio, isolated cry, no background noise. In real homes accuracy is lower. CrySnap returns a confidence score with every result and shows the top two candidates when the model is uncertain. Treat it as a strong starting point for your own intuition, not a definitive answer. Most experienced parents agree with the app most of the time; the value is the speed and the reduction of "I have no idea where to even start" paralysis.

Will my baby's audio be uploaded anywhere?

No. The classification model runs entirely on your iPhone — that is why it works at 3 a.m. with no WiFi. Audio is discarded after the result is returned. This is a privacy decision we made deliberately because baby audio is among the most sensitive household data your phone can capture. The pediatric nurse / sleep coach / lactation consultant chat features do use a cloud connection because they rely on large language models; those conversations are anonymized.

What if CrySnap returns "undefined"?

About 5-10% of cries do not classify cleanly — usually because they are mixed (a tired baby who is also hungry) or because the recording captured background noise more than the cry. When the verdict is undefined, the app walks you through the basics: hunger window, last diaper, room temperature, time since last nap. These four checks resolve most undefined cases without the model needing to be smarter.

What CrySnap is not

CrySnap is a parenting companion, not a medical device. It does not diagnose any condition. The pediatric nurse, sleep coach, and lactation consultant are AI assistants that provide reference information; they do not replace your pediatrician, your IBCLC, or your sleep consultant. For severe symptoms — fever in a newborn under 3 months, lethargy, blue lips or fingertips, breathing difficulty, projectile vomiting, refusal to feed for several hours, or any rapid change in behavior — call 911 or your pediatrician immediately. AI cry classification is not 100% accurate, and a cry app is not a baby monitor or safety device.
