What JewelSnap actually does

JewelSnap is an iOS app built on Apple's computer vision frameworks plus a jewelry-specific model trained on reference imagery across hallmarks, gemstone optics, metal patinas, and historical design conventions. Every scan returns seven structured outputs:

  • Jewelry type — ring, necklace, bracelet, brooch, earrings, pendant, or other classification
  • Material analysis — gold karat estimation (10K, 14K, 18K, 22K, 24K), sterling silver, platinum, plated metals, and alloy composition
  • Gemstone identification — diamond, ruby, sapphire, emerald, amethyst, cubic zirconia, moissanite, and synthetic stones, with inclusion-pattern analysis
  • Era dating — Georgian, Victorian, Edwardian, Art Nouveau, Art Deco, Retro, Mid-Century, Contemporary, with stylistic confidence reasoning
  • Authenticity verdict — multi-point check across hallmarks, stone optics, and material consistency, returned with a confidence score (0-100%)
  • Market value estimate — price range based on materials, craftsmanship grade, era, and current secondary-market conditions
  • Hallmark decoding — interprets stamps like 750, 585, 925, PT950, plus maker's marks from over 50 countries

Every result includes a transparent confidence rating. If the AI is uncertain, the app says so explicitly and recommends professional verification — rather than masking uncertainty behind a confident-sounding answer.

The five features that matter most

1. Era dating across major historical periods

Most jewelry identifier apps stop at material and value. JewelSnap separately classifies historical era. An Art Deco diamond ring from the 1920s can be worth five times its raw-material value because of its design pedigree. The app recognizes design signatures across Georgian (1714-1837), Victorian (1837-1901), Edwardian (1901-1910), Art Nouveau (1890-1910), Art Deco (1920-1935), Retro (1935-1950), Mid-Century (1950-1965), and Contemporary periods.

2. Hallmark decoder for 50+ countries

That tiny stamp inside the ring — 750, 585, 925, PT950, plus a maker's mark — tells you everything if you can read it. How a JewelSnap scan works

Three steps from camera to verdict, designed for the speed you need in a thrift store or estate sale.

  1. Open JewelSnap and tap the camera button

    Hold your iPhone 6-12 inches from the jewelry under decent light. The app prompts you when lighting or focus is insufficient.

  2. Take 1-3 photos (front, back, hallmark close-up)

    For best accuracy, capture the hallmark stamp if visible. The app uses macro focus on iPhone 13 Pro and later. Without a clear hallmark, accuracy on metal purity drops — but identification of stones, era, and approximate value still works.

  3. Read the verdict

    Within 15-30 seconds you get the seven structured outputs plus confidence scores. Tap any field for the AI Expert chat. Save to portfolio. Export PDF. Decide.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is JewelSnap at identifying jewelry from a photo?

JewelSnap returns a confidence score (0-100%) with every scan. For well-lit photos of clearly stamped jewelry, accuracy on metal type identification is typically above 85%. For era dating and gemstone identification, accuracy depends heavily on photo quality and the presence of identifying features (hallmarks, inclusions, design signatures).

The app explicitly flags low-confidence results. For any item potentially worth over $1,000, the recommended workflow is: scan with JewelSnap to determine whether professional appraisal is warranted, then take only the high-value items to a certified gemologist.

Can JewelSnap actually tell if my gold jewelry is real?

JewelSnap analyzes visual indicators of authenticity — hallmark stamps, color consistency, wear patterns, and craftsmanship markers. This is sufficient to distinguish solid gold from costume jewelry in the vast majority of cases. It is not sufficient to distinguish 14K from 18K with certainty, or to detect very high-quality forgeries.

For genuinely high-stakes verification — buying a $5,000 ring online, valuing a significant inheritance, or insurance documentation — physical testing by a jeweler (acid test, electronic gold tester, density analysis) remains the gold standard. JewelSnap is a first filter, not a replacement.

What's the difference between JewelSnap and a professional appraiser?

A certified gemologist holds the piece in their hands, uses calibrated equipment (refractometer, polariscope, microscope, gold testers), and produces a legally defensible valuation backed by professional liability. They charge $75-150 per item, and their valuation can be used for insurance, estate tax, and litigation.

JewelSnap analyzes a photograph using AI computer vision. It costs $4.99 per week or $29.99 per year, takes 30 seconds, and produces an estimate. The estimate is useful for decision-making — should I buy this thrift store ring, should I sort this inheritance, what's my pawnshop offer worth — but is explicitly not a substitute for certified appraisal in any high-stakes context.

Does JewelSnap work on costume jewelry?

Yes. JewelSnap identifies costume jewelry as costume, including specifying common materials (gold-plated brass, silver-plated copper, lucite, base metal). For vintage costume jewelry — Bakelite, Trifari, Coro, Hobé, Miriam Haskell — the app often identifies the era and sometimes the maker, since many vintage costume pieces have collector value.

What countries' hallmarks does JewelSnap decode?

Over 50 countries. Major coverage includes: United States, United Kingdom (London, Birmingham, Sheffield, Edinburgh, Chester, Dublin assay offices), France (Paris poinçon system), Italy (Vicenza, Arezzo, Valenza), Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Spain, Portugal, the Netherlands, Belgium, Scandinavia (Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland), Russia (kokoshnik marks), Poland, Czech Republic, Hungary, Greece, Turkey, Israel, Lebanon, Saudi Arabia, UAE, India, China, Japan, Thailand, and major South American countries (Argentina, Brazil, Mexico).
