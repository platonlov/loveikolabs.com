What makes StackSnap different

Two well-funded supplement tracker apps have hundreds of thousands of users — SuppCo and Prove It. Both show you "TrustScores" (proprietary ratings that change without explanation). Neither shows you the only number that actually matters to most people on supplements: how much money you're wasting.

The savings counter — see your spending audited in dollars

Every duplicate ingredient your stack contains, every potential overdose, every cheaper alternative for the same ingredient — quantified as actual dollars. The home screen shows "Saved this month: $87" based on what the audit identified. Most users find the first $50-$200 of potential savings within the first week of scanning their shelf.

Why this matters: the average supplement user in the U.S. spends $80-$200 per month with no clear sense of return. Most are taking 2-4 supplements containing the same active ingredient (different brands, same magnesium oxide or B12 cyanocobalamin). StackSnap doesn't tell you which supplements to take — it shows you which ones overlap, where you're overdosing, and where you're overpaying. Acting on that information is your call.

The seven features that matter most

1. Savings counter on the home screen

Quantifies money saved through identified duplicates, overdoses, cheaper alternatives, and low-evidence supplements. Updates monthly. The first feature in the supplement-tracker category to show dollars instead of scores.

2. Drug interaction checker (powered by RxNav)

RxNav is the National Library of Medicine's drug interaction reference — the same dataset pharmacists use to flag risky combinations. Add your prescriptions once, and StackSnap warns you the moment you scan a new supplement that might interact (cytochrome P450 effects, absorption competition, blood-thinning additivity, blood-pressure compounding, etc.). Always confirm flags with your pharmacist before any change.

3. NIH DSLD ingredient breakdown

Over 130,000 supplement products indexed by the U.S. National Institutes of Health. Every ingredient and amount is source-cited to the DSLD entry — no proprietary "TrustScores" that change without explanation. You can verify any data point against the public NIH database directly.

4. Smart timing reminders

Morning vitamin D, evening magnesium, berberine with food, melatonin 30 minutes before bed, fat-soluble vitamins with the largest meal. StackSnap organizes timing based on general wellness guidelines and the specific ingredient profile of what you're taking.

5. 1-page doctor-ready PDF

When your healthcare provider asks "what are you taking?" — hand them a clean, organized one-page summary. Brand, ingredient, daily amount, schedule, and any flagged interactions. Saves five minutes of fumbling at every appointment and gives your doctor real information to work with.

6. Transparent benchmark pricing

For 25 popular ingredients, StackSnap shows whether you're paying above or below the U.S. median price per serving. No more guessing whether $40 for 60 capsules of magnesium is fair. Helpful when deciding which bottles to repurchase versus which to swap for cheaper equivalents.

7. Honest 7-day free trial

Full Pro access from day one. No 20-question quiz before you see the app, no email signup gate, no fake countdown timers, no guilt screens. Cancel in two taps. The opposite of the dark-pattern paywalls that killed competing apps in this category.

How a StackSnap scan works

  1. Open StackSnap and tap the camera

    Point at the supplement bottle. Best results when both the front label (brand/name) and the supplement facts panel (ingredient list) are visible. Barcode scanning also works for products in the NIH DSLD database.

  2. Read the breakdown in 2-3 seconds

    Every active ingredient with its amount, plus context from the DSLD database. Highlighted in yellow: ingredients you're already getting from another supplement in your stack. Highlighted in red: amounts exceeding tolerable upper intake levels.

  3. Check interactions automatically

    If you've entered your prescriptions, StackSnap checks the new supplement against the RxNav database immediately. Any flagged interaction shows up with the documented mechanism and severity.

  4. Save to your stack, set timing

    One tap saves the supplement to your tracked stack with default timing (morning, evening, with food). Tap to customize. The savings counter updates if the scan revealed duplicates or overdoses.

The math of an audited supplement stack

Concrete example from an actual stack audit:

  • Multivitamin ($25/mo) — already contains 50% of the magnesium in your separate magnesium glycinate ($18/mo). Overlap identified — potential reduction $9/mo.
  • Two B12 products — your B-complex contains 100% RDA and your separate B12 sublingual contains 16,000% RDA. Combined intake is far above what your body can absorb. One can come out — $14/mo saved.
  • Generic store-brand magnesium oxide at $40 for 60 capsules. Benchmark pricing shows median $14 for equivalent dose. Cheaper alternative identified — $26/mo saved.
  • $60/mo nootropic "memory blend" with no peer-reviewed evidence for the stated combination. StackSnap flags low-evidence supplements (citing absence of ingredient-specific studies). User's choice whether to keep — but at least the information is there.

Total identified potential monthly savings from one audit: $49 confirmed plus $60 if user chooses to drop the unproven blend. The annual subscription ($39.99) pays for itself in the first month.
